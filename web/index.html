How to take a photo that works
  • Take the card out of its sleeve. A snug sleeve edge can be mistaken for the card edge and throw the answer off by 0.3mm.
  • Put it on plain white paper. That gives the clearest possible edge. A plain dark mat works too.
  • Soft light from all sides. One lamp, or a window off to one side, casts a shadow along the card edge that can move the answer by up to 0.7mm. Bounce the light off a wall or ceiling.
  • Straight down, card flat. A small tilt is fine and gets corrected. Flatten any curl first, especially on foils.
  • Leave a gap around the card. Fill most of the frame, but keep a clear margin between the card and the edge of the photo - phone lenses bend the picture out there.

Scans and the cropped images grading companies send out also work, with no extra steps.

Drag a box around the card
It could not find the card on its own. Drag a rough box along the card's own edges. If the card is sleeved, follow where the card meets the sleeve, not the outer edge of the sleeve. Being exact does not matter — the box only says where to look, and the measuring is done the same way as always.
